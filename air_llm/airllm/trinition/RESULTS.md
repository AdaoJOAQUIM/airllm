# Falsification results — Trinition / Atangana operators

These are real numbers from `benchmark.py` (seed 0), not claims. Reproduce with:

```bash
cd air_llm/airllm && python -c "import trinition.benchmark as b; b.main(['--seed','0'])"
```

The harness compares composition **algebras at strictly equal readout-parameter
budgets** on two synthetic tasks. Only the algebra changes, so any gap is
attributable to the algebra and nothing else. Ground truth is generated by exact
mathematics, independent of any model.

## Task A — SO(3) rotation composition (control)

Target: the exact composed unit quaternion. Quaternions are the *known-correct*
algebra here, so this task is a control: it checks the harness can detect
"algebra matched to structure". Test MSE (lower better); every head has 20 params.

| model \ N   | 50 | 200 | 800 | 3200 |
|-------------|------|------|------|------|
| real (diag) | 0.2938 | 0.2166 | 0.2060 | 0.2043 |
| quaternion  | **0.0000** | **0.0000** | **0.0000** | **0.0000** |
| trinition   | 0.2707 | 0.2107 | 0.2047 | 0.2033 |

**Deformation sweep** — the thesis's central claim is that the Trinition product
is *deformable* and can be tuned to fit the structure. Given that knob a fair
shot on Task A (N=3200):

| alpha | 0 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.75 | 1 | 1.5 | 2 |
|-------|------|------|------|------|------|------|------|
| MSE   | 0.2038 | 0.2039 | 0.2039 | 0.2040 | 0.2041 | 0.2045 | 0.2047 |

Best alpha = 0 → MSE 0.2038, vs quaternion control 0.0000.

**Verdict on Task A:** the deformability knob does essentially nothing here. No
setting of the Trinition algebra gets within two orders of magnitude of the
matched algebra; Trinition lands right next to the *wrong* (real-diagonal)
baseline. For rotation-structured data, "swap matrices for a deformable
Trinition product and win" is **falsified**. The win comes from matching the
algebra to the data's symmetry (quaternion ≅ SO(3)), not from hypercomplexity
per se — consistent with the Geometric Deep Learning result that the only known
free lunch is equivariance to a symmetry actually present in the data.

## Task B — fractional-memory process (non-Markovian, α = 0.5)

A long-memory process where an Atangana-style fractional operator is *expected*
to help, if anywhere. Test MSE; #params shown per model.

| model \ N | 50 | 200 | 800 | 3200 |
|-----------|------|------|------|------|
| fractional (α=0.5, 2 feat) **[3p]** | 1.0857 | 0.9858 | 0.9760 | **0.9766** |
| real AR(k=2) [3p] | 1.0547 | 1.0022 | 1.0164 | 0.9927 |
| real AR(k=4) [5p] | 1.1007 | 1.0186 | 0.9915 | 0.9891 |
| real AR(k=8) [9p] | 1.2120 | 1.0053 | 0.9891 | 0.9807 |

**Verdict on Task B:** the 3-parameter fractional head (0.9766) edges out even
the 9-parameter real AR(8) (0.9807) at the largest sample size — a genuine, if
modest, parameter-efficiency win. This is the one place the Atangana operator
earns its keep: long-memory, non-Markovian structure encoded cheaply by a single
fractional-derivative feature.

## Overall read-out

- The grand thesis ("LLMs lose because of the wrong algebra; deformable Trinition
  is a paradigm shift") is **not supported**. Where an algebra helps, it helps
  because it matches a symmetry in the data — and the default/deformed Trinition
  product did not match the one task built to reward it.
- The **fractional-calculus** half of Atangana's work shows a real, reproducible
  (modest) edge on long-memory data. That is a publishable, useful, bounded
  result — an *added tool*, not a destroyed industry.
- This is two synthetic tasks, not a proof. To strengthen either direction:
  (1) *learn* the structure constants by gradient descent instead of sweeping one
  scalar — the honest strongest form of the deformability claim; (2) test on a
  symmetry Trinition is actually suited to, if one can be identified; (3) scale
  Task B to real long-memory time series. The harness is built to extend.
